    Simulation notes

    How to demonstrate the application
    1. Start with normal operation and observe the sensor values.
    2. Activate one fault and explain how the readings change.
    3. Show the diagnosis, control recommendation, and event log.
    4. Disable automatic control to demonstrate why independent PLC trips matter.

    Project description · assignment response

    Problem & objective

    A mixing tank can overheat, become over-pressurized, or develop agitator vibration. The simulator monitors four sensor signals, detects deviations, and illustrates automatic actions that can reduce process risk and downtime.

    System design

    Temperature, pressure, level, and vibration sensors feed a condition-monitoring layer. A diagnosis agent links abnormal patterns to likely faults; a control agent proposes bounded adjustments; an independent safety layer checks trip conditions.

    Decision examples

    High temperature: switch off normal heating and open cooling. Rising pressure: close feed and open the relief path. High vibration: reduce agitator speed. Critical readings: latch a controlled stop and raise an operator alert.

    Safety and scope: This is an educational software simulation with illustrative thresholds and simplified process dynamics. It is not connected to equipment and must not be used to control a real plant. In an actual installation, certified PLC logic, interlocks, risk assessment, and human operating procedures remain necessary. The AI agents support monitoring and recommendations; they do not replace safety-rated controls.
